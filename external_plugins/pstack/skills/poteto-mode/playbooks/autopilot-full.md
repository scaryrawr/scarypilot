### Autopilot-full

**You own the verdicts, never the PRs. One owner runs each PR from build to merge, and nothing merges without your clean swarm verdict.** For "autopilot this queue", "full autopilot", and one-owner-per-PR programs. Orchestrate runs a standing program whose coordinator lands verified work itself and whose workers never merge. Here each PR's owner carries the whole lifecycle through the merge, and the root keeps only verification, countersigns, and audits.

1. **Mark the operator's items and honor state-then-wait.** Items the operator names stay with the operator. The operator reviews and clicks, and no owner merges one. When the operator asks for the protocol or the plan to be stated, deliver the statement and stop. Execution starts only on the operator's explicit go. On that go, persist the full program objective in the decision trail. Re-read it at every audit until the queue is done.
2. **Spawn one owner per PR with the full lifecycle and an early trail.** One background Copilot agent in an isolated worktree or project session per PR owns build, first push, a ready PR, self-proof on the real artifact (the **prove-it-works** principle skill), skeptical Bugbot triage per `../references/bugbot-triage.md`, a slop-strip through `/deslop` and `/unslop`, `/no-comments`, a rebase onto current trunk, the babysit loop to green (`playbooks/babysit.md`), and the merge itself. Use **Opening a PR** for host tools and forge operations; do not require Graphite for independent PRs. Within about 15 minutes, every owner starts a `decisions.tsv` trail, pushes a first branch snapshot, and opens its PR before self-proof. After that, push after every verifiable unit with hooks on. Keep the trail uncommitted and return it with reports. Track child agent IDs, expected runtimes, and states in a separate `children.tsv`. The owner rebases before the code-ready report and babysit, whether or not trunk has drifted. In fix rounds, keep that merge base. Rebase again only at merge prep, on a conflict with trunk, or on a CI failure caused by trunk. After the slop-strip and `/no-comments`, report the code-ready head SHA and each later push that changes the patch. Self-proof, CI, and babysit run in parallel with the swarm. Report merge-ready with the head SHA when they finish. Before a PR-facing push, run the repository's pre-review checks on the committed head. A hook pass is not self-proof. Publish a rebase only to the owner's own branch with `git push --force-with-lease` after an `ls-remote` check. Never force-push a shared branch. The merge is the one step an owner may not take alone; step 4 gates it.
3. **Run owners in true parallel and never stack.** Many owners at once when PRs are self-contained: one writer per branch, disjoint files, cross-PR drift absorbed by rebase. Only genuinely overlapping work serializes. Self-contained PRs branch straight off main, and sequenced work is merge-then-branch. One exception: an owner that must split a genuinely dependent change may hold a short private base-branch stack.
4. **Swarm-verify every round before its merge.** A round starts at the owner's code-ready head SHA and again at each later push that changes the PR's patch. At that SHA, fan out parallel independent verifiers per the **swarm** skill. The fan-out mechanics live there; do not restate them. The early lanes: re-run the gates at that SHA; prove the load-bearing behavior live on the real surface the change touches (the project verification skill or available UI/CLI tools); audit the diff only, distrusting the PR body. These lane results are provisional while the owner's self-proof is still running. After the owner reports merge-ready, the root separately audits that report and its final self-proof, CI, and babysit receipts against the reported head SHA and required pass predicates. Missing, stale, or failed evidence blocks a clean verdict. Aggregate and accept a clean verdict only after this receipt audit passes and all required lanes pass or remain valid for that head under the patch-id rule in `playbooks/shipping.md`. The live lane is the floor, and a verdict without it is not clean. No merge without the root's clean verdict. Findings go back to the owner for fix-forward, and the new head gets a fresh swarm and a fresh receipt audit, except for lane results that stay valid under the shipping rule.
5. **On a clean verdict the owner merges, and a fresh owner takes the next item.** Merge prep follows the round's lanes and rebases onto current trunk. Report the new head SHA and updated merge-ready receipts. CI must pass on that head, and the root repeats the receipt audit from step 4. Revalidate lanes under the patch-id rule in `playbooks/shipping.md` after every rewritten head. Once that head is green and its patch matches the verdict, a later trunk move alone does not force another rebase. Immediately before merge, fetch trunk and check that `git merge-tree` of the head against current trunk is clean. Compare trunk changes since the merge base against the PR's changed paths and the paths that select its CI, including repository CI configuration. An overlap or conflict requires another rebase, fresh CI, updated receipts, and lane revalidation before repeating the checks. The owner squash-merges its own PR and returns. A fresh owner takes the next independent item with a complete brief. The operator's full-autonomy grant plus the root's clean verdict is the merge authorization that babysitting alone never has. Operator-named items stop at merge-ready and wait for the operator's click.
6. **Run the root layer on an hourly audit tick.** A genuinely new raise of a pinned gate or budget value (a limit CI only lets tighten) needs your fresh countersign, granted only after verifier proof. Never bypass forge-enforced approvals. Absorbing values that already landed on main is drift, not a raise. On the operator's go, schedule the hourly audit with the host's session automation when available and approved. Otherwise use one bounded timer while the session remains active and state that it cannot wake a closed session. Process completion notifications as queue events; do not poll or rely on memory for the cadence. At each audit, re-read this playbook and the persisted objective. Probe known running owners with `read_agent` or the host's session status tool and collect their decision trails. Count only side effects as progress: commits, pushes, PR or check deltas, and store reports. Check the pushed branches and `children.tsv` too. Treat errors, inability to start a turn, or exceeding the expected runtime without a side effect as stuck. Record the gap and use available stop or hold controls before dispatching a replacement; do not allow two writers on the same branch. A stall never proves or drops the work. When merges batch, run a retro pass and a post-merge bot-comment sweep. Stop the audit schedule when no delegated work remains, including after the last merge.
7. **Stand down instantly on the operator's stop.** The operator's hold or stand-down reaches every owner as a zero-writes order immediately. Owners hold their briefs until the operator releases them.

**Reply:** the queue with each PR's owner, state, and head SHA. Each verdict and the swarm that produced it. What merged and what each fresh owner took next. Countersigns granted and why. Open operator gates. Where the collected decision trails live.
